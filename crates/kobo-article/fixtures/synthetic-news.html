Harbour wall to be rebuilt after winter storms

The council has approved a two-year plan to replace the damaged stretch of the east wall.

By Mara Lindqvist 14 March 2026
Share on X Share on Facebook Email

The east wall of the harbour will be rebuilt over the next two years after the council approved the plan at a meeting on Thursday evening, ending months of uncertainty for the fishing fleet that moors behind it.

Engineers found that a forty metre stretch of the wall had been undermined during the storms in February, when waves of more than six metres were recorded at the harbour mouth for three nights in a row.

Advertisement

"We have been patching this wall for twenty years," said the harbour master, Tomas Ekberg. "This time the sea has made the decision for us, and I would rather we did the job properly once."

What the work involves

The plan replaces the damaged section with a new wall built on piles driven into the bedrock, rather than on the rubble foundations laid in the nineteenth century. The work will be done in three phases so that the harbour can stay open throughout.

  • Phase one: temporary sheet piling along the damaged section, starting in May.
  • Phase two: demolition of the old wall and construction of the new foundations.
  • Phase three: the new wall face, mooring rings and lighting, finishing in spring 2028.

The cost of the scheme is put at 11.4 million pounds, of which 8 million will come from a national coastal resilience fund. The council will borrow the rest over thirty years.

The fleet cannot move to another harbour for two years. Whatever we do has to keep the boats working.

Fishing crews said they were relieved that a decision had been made, but worried about the noise and disruption of piling work during the summer, when the harbour is busiest with visitors.
