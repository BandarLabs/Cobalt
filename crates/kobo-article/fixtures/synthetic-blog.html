Measuring tide heights with a pressure sensor

Posted on 21 September 2025 by Sam Okafor · 6 min read

Our slipway floods at the top of every spring tide, and the published tide tables are for a port twelve miles up the coast. I wanted to know how far off they are for our bit of shore, so I spent a weekend building a logger from a pressure sensor and a microcontroller I had in a drawer.

#How it works

A sealed pressure sensor sits in a weighted pipe at the bottom of the slipway. Water pressure rises by about one hundred pascals for every centimetre of depth, so the reading minus the air pressure gives the height of water above the sensor.

Parts

  1. A sealed pressure sensor rated to two bar.
  2. A small microcontroller board with an SD card slot.
  3. Four metres of cable, a length of drainpipe and some lead weights.

#The code

The logger wakes once a minute, takes ten readings, and writes the median to the card. Taking the median rather than the mean throws away the spikes that waves put on the reading.

// Ten readings, keep the middle one.
fn median(mut readings: [u32; 10]) -> u32 {
    readings.sort_unstable();
    readings[5]
}

The depth is then (water - air) / 98 millimetres, close enough for this job, where air comes from a second sensor kept in the shed.

#Results

Over three weeks the logger recorded forty high waters. Our high water comes on average eighteen minutes after the published time, and the spring tides run eleven centimetres higher than the table says.

TidePublishedMeasuredDifference
Spring5.20 m5.31 m+11 cm
Neap3.90 m3.94 m+4 cm

The table is right about everything except the bit of shore you are standing on.

Next I want to add a temperature sensor and see whether the sea warms faster on the flood than on the ebb, which the fishermen here swear it does.
